BD-HeatPred: A Multi-Source Daily Predictor Dataset for Heatwave Research in Bangladesh, February–June, 1991–2025

Nafiul Islam Naim | Tawyabul Islam Tamim | Suchi Chaki

Status: the dataset has been assembled, and quality control and technical validation are in progress. It is not yet publicly released. This page will be updated with download and citation details at release.

Overview

BD-HeatPred is a daily dataset for analyzing heatwaves in Bangladesh. For each of five climatically distinct regions, it brings together 35 atmospheric, land-surface, ocean and climate-mode variables from seven sources.

Dataset at a glance

Regions
Dhaka, Khulna, Rajshahi and Rangpur divisions, and the Rangamati, Bandarban and Khagrachari hill districts (RBK). Ocean variables cover the Bay of Bengal.
Period
1991–2025 (35 years)
Year window
1 February – 30 June
Temporal resolution
Daily (local day, UTC+6), built from hourly data where needed
Spatial resolution
Regional area means, computed from source grids of 0.1° to 2.5°
Variables
35 predictors
Size
5,259 daily records per region
Format
CSV, one file per region

Why this dataset?

Pre-monsoon heatwaves are a serious hazard in Bangladesh. April and May are the hottest months, and daily maxima of 36–40 °C are common in the northwest and southwest. Heatwaves are linked to several processes: dry air and subsidence aloft, dry soils, moisture transport from the Bay of Bengal, and large-scale modes such as the MJO and the Indian Ocean Dipole.

The data needed to study these processes together are scattered. They sit in different archives (reanalyses, satellite records, index products) with different grids, time steps, time zones and file formats. Each research group has to download, clip, align and derive the variables again. That takes effort, adds room for error, and makes results hard to compare or reproduce.

BD-HeatPred does this preparation once, with documented steps. It provides analysis-ready daily tables for each region, all on the same calendar and window.

seven source

What's new

  • One aligned table from seven sources. Atmosphere, land surface, Bay of Bengal ocean conditions and climate modes share a single daily calendar for each region.
  • Built for heatwaves. Variables were chosen from the literature on Bangladesh heatwaves and cover heat, dryness, circulation, land–atmosphere coupling and remote drivers. The February–June window brackets the heat season.
  • Regional, not national. Five regions with different climates are kept separate, so regional differences in heatwave drivers can be studied.
  • Physically derived predictors. Vapour-pressure deficit, 850 hPa wind speed, moisture-flux convergence and temperature advection are computed from hourly fields, with daily mean, minimum and maximum. VPD is computed hourly because it depends non-linearly on temperature, so a daily mean of hourly values differs from VPD computed from daily-mean inputs.
  • Daily vegetation and water indices. NDVI and NDWI observations are only available every few days. They are reconstructed to daily values with a weighted Whittaker smoother.
  • Anomalies on a standard base period. Anomalies of Bay of Bengal OLR and SST, and the Dipole Mode Index, use the WMO 1991–2020 climate normal.

Variables

The 35 predictors fall into eight groups.

Group Variables Scale
Tropospheric temperature Temperature at 850 and 500 hPa Regional
Humidity Relative and specific humidity at 850 and 700 hPa Regional
Evaporative demand (derived) Vapour-pressure deficit: daily mean, min, max Regional
Circulation Wind components at 850 and 200 hPa, 500 hPa geopotential, 500 hPa vertical velocity Regional
Transport diagnostics (derived) 850 hPa wind speed, moisture-flux convergence and temperature advection: daily mean, min, max each Regional
Land surface Surface soil moisture (0–7 cm), NDVI, NDWI Regional
Bay of Bengal ocean & convection SST and SST anomaly, OLR and OLR anomaly Basin
Climate modes MJO (RMM1, RMM2, amplitude), Dipole Mode Index Global / basin

27 variables differ by region. The 8 basin- and global-scale variables are identical in every regional file.
